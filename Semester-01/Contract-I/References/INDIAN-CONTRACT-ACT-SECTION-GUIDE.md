# K-1005 — Indian Contract Act, 1872 Section-Wise Learning Guide

**The table is a topic map, not verbatim statutory text.** Check the current Act from India Code before quoting sections.

| Section(s) | Heading/subject | Study point |
|---|---|---|
| 2(a)–(h) | Proposal, acceptance, consideration, agreement, void/voidable/contract definitions | Learn the statutory vocabulary; an agreement is not automatically a contract |
| 3–6 | Communication, completion and revocation of proposal/acceptance | Timing and notice matter; analyse the communication method |
| 7–9 | Absolute acceptance, performance acceptance, express/implied promise | Counter-offer and acceptance by conduct |
| 10 | What agreements are contracts | Free consent, competence, lawful consideration/object and not expressly void; subject to applicable law |
| 11–12 | Capacity and sound mind | Majority, sound mind at the relevant time and no legal disqualification |
| 13–14 | Consent and free consent | Same thing in same sense; list the vitiating factors |
| 15–16 | Coercion; undue influence | Distinguish unlawful threat/property detention from domination of will/unfair advantage |
| 17–18 | Fraud; misrepresentation | Intent and statutory category matter |
| 19, 19A | Voidability and undue influence remedy | Identify whose option it is and the applicable conditions |
| 20–22 | Mistake | Bilateral essential fact, mistake of law, unilateral fact mistake |
| 23–24 | Lawful consideration/object; partly unlawful agreement | Read the exact categories and separability rule |
| 25 | No consideration, statutory exceptions | Check writing/registration or other requirements for the invoked exception |
| 26–30 | Restraint of marriage/trade/legal proceedings, uncertainty, wager | Each section has a distinct test and exceptions |
| 31–36 | Contingent contracts | Event, collateral nature, happening/non-happening, impossibility/time |
| 37–55 | Performance, joint promises, time and reciprocal promises | Identify who must perform, when and under what conditions |
| 56 | Impossible acts and supervening impossibility/frustration | Hardship alone is not enough; examine risk allocation and legal test |
| 62–63 | Novation/rescission/alteration; remission/dispensation | Identify the parties' agreement or waiver |
| 64–67 | Effects after rescission/voidness; facilities for performance | Restitution and performance conditions |
| 68 | Necessaries to person incapable of contracting | Reimbursement from property, not ordinary personal contract liability |
| 69–72 | Quasi-contractual obligations | Payment of another's debt, non-gratuitous benefit, finder, mistaken/coerced delivery |
| 73–75 | Compensation | Breach, causation, remoteness, reasonable compensation, rightful rescission |

## Must-learn case map
- *Lalman Shukla v Gauri Dutt* — knowledge of offer.
- *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* — unilateral offer/advertisement context.
- *Felthouse v Bindley* — silence not ordinarily acceptance.
- *Durga Prasad v Baldeo* — act must be at promisor's desire.
- *Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose* — minor's agreement.
- *Chinnaya v Ramayya* — consideration may move from a third person under Indian statutory wording; assess privity separately.
- *Central Inland Water Transport Corp v Brojo Nath Ganguly* — unconscionable term context.
- *Satyabrata Ghose v Mugneeram Bangur & Co* — section 56/frustration.
- *State of West Bengal v B.K. Mondal & Sons* — statutory non-gratuitous benefit/quasi-contract context.
- *Hadley v Baxendale* — remoteness as an English authority; apply Indian section 73.

## Fast decision sequence
Offer? → valid acceptance? → consideration? → capacity? → free consent? → lawful object? → void/voidable rule? → performance/discharge/breach? → remedy?
