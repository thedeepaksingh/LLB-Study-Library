# K-1003 — Tort Doctrine and Consumer Statute Guide

## Core doctrinal checklist
- General tort: legal right/duty, breach or actionable infringement, causation, damage where required, remedy and defence.
- *Damnum sine injuria*: actual loss without infringement of a legally protected right.
- *Injuria sine damnum*: legal-right infringement even without measurable loss where the tort is actionable on that basis.
- Negligence: duty → breach → causation → remoteness/legal causation → damage.
- Nuisance: substantial and unreasonable interference; distinguish public/private.
- Trespass: direct invasion/interference, subject to the particular tort's elements.
- Defamation: defamatory meaning → reference to claimant → publication → applicable fault standard → defences.
- Vicarious liability: relationship plus sufficient connection with scope/course of employment.
- *Rylands* strict liability: dangerous thing, relevant use/accumulation, escape, damage and applicable exceptions.
- Indian absolute liability: hazardous/inherently dangerous enterprise and the *M.C. Mehta* principle.
- Defences: volenti, necessity, inevitable accident, Act of God, statutory authority; select only if facts support the legal ingredients.

## Consumer Protection Act, 2019: selected statutory provisions
These descriptions are summaries rather than verbatim statutory text.
- Section 2(6): complaint.
- Section 2(7): consumer.
- Section 2(10): defect.
- Section 2(11): deficiency.
- Section 2(47): unfair trade practice.
- Sections 34, 47 and 58: District, State and National Commission jurisdiction framework, subject to the 2021 jurisdiction rules.
- Sections 82–87: product-liability chapter provisions; consult all provisions for the exact claim.
- Section 100: Act's additional-remedy clause (check the exact current text and interaction with other law before reliance).

## Jurisdiction thresholds in the 2021 Rules
Based on the value of goods/services paid as consideration:
- District Commission: up to and including ₹50 lakh.
- State Commission: above ₹50 lakh and up to and including ₹2 crore.
- National Commission: above ₹2 crore.

Primary Gazette source: [Department of Consumer Affairs — jurisdiction rules PDF](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/jurisdicton%20of%20Consumer%20Commissions.pdf). Recheck for amendments.

## Cases to connect with doctrine
- *Ashby v White*: legal-right infringement without actual loss.
- *Gloucester Grammar School Case*: loss from lawful competition without legal wrong.
- *Donoghue v Stevenson*: duty of care.
- *Rylands v Fletcher*: strict liability.
- *M.C. Mehta v Union of India* (Oleum Gas Leak): Indian absolute liability.
- *State of Rajasthan v Vidyawati*: State liability in the case's context.
- *Kasturi Lal Ralia Ram Jain v State of U.P.*: historical sovereign-function approach.
- *N. Nagendra Rao & Co. v State of A.P.*: later limitation of broad immunity.
- *Lucknow Development Authority v M.K. Gupta*: consumer protection and public authority/services.
- *Indian Medical Association v V.P. Shantha*: consumer-service doctrine in the medical-services context; use only after checking the current statutory context.

## Study warning
The law of torts has evolved through common-law decisions and statutes; do not assume an old English rule applies without qualification in India. In consumer problems use the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and applicable rules first where relevant.
