# K-1003 — Original Practice Questions and Worked Answers
**Original exercises, not verified CCSU previous-year questions.**

## Part A — Long-answer plans

### PRACTICE-TORT-01. Define tort and explain its essential characteristics.
Define tort as a civil wrong addressed by civil remedies; discuss duties generally fixed by law, remedy, and the difference from contract/crime; explain constituents while noting that elements vary by tort.

### PRACTICE-TORT-02. Distinguish damnum sine injuria from injuria sine damnum.
Define both phrases, accurately distinguish loss from legal injury, use the competition example and *Ashby v White*, and conclude that tort law protects legal rights rather than every setback.

### PRACTICE-TORT-03. Explain the rules of strict and absolute liability.
Describe *Rylands* elements and traditional exceptions; describe the Indian *M.C. Mehta* absolute-liability principle for hazardous enterprise; compare and explain why the labels are not interchangeable.

### PRACTICE-TORT-04. Explain volenti non fit injuria and other justifications.
Explain free and informed consent to a risk; distinguish knowledge from consent; discuss necessity, inevitable accident, Act of God and statutory authority with their own requirements.

### PRACTICE-TORT-05. Discuss vicarious liability.
Explain employment relationship and course/scope of employment; distinguish authorised work done wrongfully from purely private acts; discuss employee v independent contractor and exceptions.

### PRACTICE-TORT-06. Explain State liability and sovereign immunity in India.
Set out the historical distinction; discuss *Vidyawati*, *Kasturi Lal* and *N. Nagendra Rao* in their respective contexts; distinguish tort damages from constitutional compensation.

### PRACTICE-TORT-07. Explain the elements of negligence.
State duty, breach, causation, remoteness and damage; explain the reasonable-care standard and *Donoghue v Stevenson*; apply rather than assume liability from a bad outcome.

### PRACTICE-TORT-08. Distinguish public and private nuisance.
Define each; explain who has standing, substantial/unreasonable interference and possible remedies.

### PRACTICE-TORT-09. Explain trespass and defamation.
Separate the elements of trespass to land/person from defamation; discuss consent/justification and truth, opinion or privilege defences as applicable.

### PRACTICE-TORT-10. Explain consumer rights and redress under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
Cover consumer status; defect/deficiency/unfair practice; complaint and remedies; product liability; the current forum threshold rules and their consideration-based measure.

## Part B — Fact patterns

### Problem 1: Harmful competition
A new shop opens legally near an existing shop. The original shop loses customers, but there is no deception, trespass or violation of a specific legal restriction.
**Worked answer:** The original shop has suffered economic loss, but the facts do not establish legal-right infringement. This suggests *damnum sine injuria*. Lawful competition is generally not tortious merely because it reduces profit. If there were deception, unlawful interference, passing off or another independent wrong, the analysis could change.

### Problem 2: A denied right
An authorised voter is wrongly prevented from voting, but the candidate wins by a large majority.
**Worked answer:** A large margin does not erase the legal right. Analyse *injuria sine damnum*: the legally protected voting right may have been infringed even if the claimant cannot show measurable loss. The cause of action and remedy depend on the governing legal process and facts.

### Problem 3: Industrial leak
A company stores a hazardous chemical for an industrial process, and it escapes beyond the premises, injuring neighbours.
**Worked answer:** First consider the traditional *Rylands* requirements—dangerous thing, relevant land use, escape and damage—then test its exceptions. Because a hazardous enterprise is involved, also consider the Indian absolute-liability doctrine in *M.C. Mehta*. Do not stop at the word “strict”; identify the doctrinal route and relevant facts.

### Problem 4: Negligence and an unforeseeable outcome
A shopkeeper mops a floor, leaves no warning sign, and a customer slips. The parties dispute whether the floor was wet and whether the customer was looking where they walked.
**Worked answer:** Apply duty, breach, causation, remoteness and damage. Evidence about the floor and warnings helps establish breach; the customer's conduct may be relevant to contributory negligence depending on the jurisdictional doctrine. The injury alone does not prove breach. If the facts are unclear, explain what evidence is required.

### Problem 5: Employee acts during assigned work
A delivery employee negligently injures a pedestrian while making a delivery for the employer. The employer argues the employee alone is responsible.
**Worked answer:** Consider vicarious liability. The key is whether the employee relationship and course/scope connection are established. Conduct in performing assigned delivery work may fall within employment even if done negligently. Verify the employer relationship and exact circumstances; a purely personal departure may change the outcome.

### Problem 6: Public authority and property
A public authority seizes goods under legal authority, then stores them negligently and they are damaged.
**Worked answer:** Identify the statutory power, the duty of care, the employee conduct and the nature of the governmental function. The fact that an authority acted under statute does not automatically answer liability. Use *Kasturi Lal* as historical context and *N. Nagendra Rao* for the later restriction on broad immunity; consider statutory remedies and the precise facts.

### Problem 7: Repeated loud noise
A factory operates machinery through the night in a residential area, repeatedly preventing residents from sleeping.
**Worked answer:** The facts may support private nuisance if there is substantial and unreasonable interference with use/enjoyment of land. Consider duration, intensity, locality, time, statutory licences and evidence. A licence may be relevant but not necessarily a blanket defence. Remedies may include injunction or damages depending on the proven claim.

### Problem 8: Online accusation
A person posts a false factual accusation about a named shopkeeper to a public group; others read and share it.
**Worked answer:** Consider defamatory meaning, identification, publication and applicable fault/defence rules. A statement's falsity and the reputational context matter, but the correct civil or criminal cause of action must be identified separately. If the statement is opinion, truthfully reports proceedings or falls under a recognised privilege, that may alter the analysis.

### Problem 9: Defective goods
A consumer buys a household appliance for personal use. The appliance has a manufacturing defect and causes damage. The seller and manufacturer deny all responsibility.
**Worked answer:** Check consumer status under CPA 2019 section 2(7), evidence of defect, the complaint ground, product-liability chapter and the role of seller/manufacturer. Identify harm and causation, statutory grounds/defences, limitation and the proper commission under current rules. Do not assume all claims against every seller succeed automatically.

### Problem 10: Paid and free services
A claimant used a service provided under a mixed paid/free arrangement and alleges deficiency.
**Worked answer:** Determine the actual arrangement and who paid; apply the CPA definition of consumer and service, the exclusion for free service or contract of personal service, and relevant case law. The label “free” or “paid” is not enough on its own. Identify the consideration and contractual structure.
