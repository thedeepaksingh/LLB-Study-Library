# DAY-06 — Detailed Lesson: State, Public Tranquillity, Women and Children

**Study identifier:** DAY-06  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Status:** Organised study outline. Use the exact current statutory provisions and the student's applicable CCSU syllabus.

## 1. Offences affecting sovereignty, unity and integrity of India

The syllabus map identifies an offence concerning acts endangering India's sovereignty, unity and integrity. Learn the exact wording of the applicable BNS section, its ingredients and any statutory explanation. A correct exam answer should analyse the legal elements rather than rely on a broad description or political opinion.

## 2. Unlawful assembly

BNS Section 189 addresses unlawful assembly. An answer should identify the required number of persons, the common object specified by the section and the other legal conditions. The assessment must be based on the statute and evidence.

Section 190 concerns liability of members for specified offences connected to the common object or known likelihood, subject to its statutory conditions. Do not assume that membership creates automatic responsibility for every act committed by any member.

## 3. Rioting and affray

Rioting and affray are distinct concepts. Read the relevant statutory definitions and identify the requirements for each, including the role of force or violence, the number and conduct of persons, and whether the statutory setting is satisfied. Avoid using the everyday meaning alone.

## 4. Offences against women and children

The located syllabus map mentions rape, sexual harassment, voyeurism, stalking, dowry death and offences against children. Each topic should be studied from the actual BNS provisions:
- definition and section;
- essential ingredients;
- statutory explanations, exceptions or qualifications;
- punishment provision;
- special age/circumstance rules where applicable;
- relevant verified case law.

Some offences may have complex statutory conditions. Do not shorten a definition so much that a required ingredient or exception is lost.

## How to prepare a specific-offence answer

1. State the correct offence and section.
2. Explain the act or omission required.
3. Identify the mental element where specified.
4. Explain circumstances, victim category, age or relationship requirements where relevant.
5. List any relevant exception or qualification.
6. State punishment only after verifying the applicable subsection.
7. Apply facts and conclude.

## Case-law anchors for this lesson
- **Lalji v State of Uttar Pradesh, (1989) 1 SCC 437:** unlawful-assembly liability may apply without proof that every member personally performed an overt act, but membership and statutory common-object conditions must be proved.
- **Masalti v State of Uttar Pradesh, AIR 1965 SC 202:** group-offence cases require careful assessment of identification, membership and the applicable statutory conditions.
- **State of Punjab v Gurmit Singh, (1996) 2 SCC 384:** sexual-offence evidence must be evaluated sensitively and without a rigid assumption that corroboration is always required.
- **Satbir Singh v State of Haryana, (2021) 6 SCC 1:** “soon before” in the former dowry-death framework is contextual and requires a live/proximate link, not a fixed rule of immediate timing.
- All cases are legacy authorities. Apply current BNS provisions, particularly sections 152, 189–194 and 63–99. Do not call section 152 a simple re-numbering of former IPC section 124A.
