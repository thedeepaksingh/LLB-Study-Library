# Model Answer 03 — Common Intention and Common Object

**Question classification:** Predicted/model question; not a verified PYQ.

## Introduction

The BNS contains rules for attributing liability when several persons are involved in an offence. Common intention and common object are different legal concepts, and the proper rule depends on the evidence and statutory requirements.

## Common intention

Section 3(5) concerns a criminal act done by several persons in furtherance of the common intention of all. Where the provision applies, each person may be liable in the manner stated in the section. An answer should examine whether a common intention existed, who participated and whether the act was done in furtherance of that intention.

## Common object

Section 189 addresses unlawful assembly. In general, the statutory definition requires five or more persons and one of the common objects identified by the law. Section 190 deals with liability of members for certain offences committed in prosecution of the common object or known to be likely, subject to its wording.

## Main differences

| Common intention | Common object |
|---|---|
| Section 3(5) | Sections 189–190 |
| Focuses on shared intention and an act in furtherance of it | Operates through the law of unlawful assembly |
| No five-person minimum appears in Section 3(5) itself | Unlawful assembly generally requires five or more persons |
| The role and participation of each person matter | Membership, common object and the statutory connection to the offence matter |

## Conclusion

Both doctrines concern group liability, but they have distinct ingredients. The conclusion must be based on the correct statutory provision and facts; mere presence or association is not a substitute for the legal test.
