# Model Answer 01 — Define Crime and Explain Its Essential Elements

**Question classification:** Predicted/model question; not a verified PYQ.  
**Topic:** General principles of crime.

## Introduction

A crime is an act or omission prohibited by law and punishable by the State. A person is not criminally liable merely because their conduct is morally wrong; the legal ingredients of a defined offence must be established.

## External element — actus reus

Actus reus is a general term for the external element of an offence. Depending on the offence, it may include an act, a legally relevant omission, particular circumstances or a result. The prosecution must establish the external element required by the statutory definition.

## Mental element — mens rea

Mens rea refers to the mental element required for the particular offence. Depending on the provision, it may involve intention, knowledge, dishonesty, rashness or another legally relevant state of mind. The required mental element differs from offence to offence.

The general concepts of actus reus and mens rea are useful, but they must not be applied as a rigid formula to every offence. Some statutory offences modify the ordinary mental-element requirement.

## Other legal requirements

The prosecution must prove the remaining ingredients of the offence. Any applicable general exception or defence must be assessed under the statutory conditions.

## Illustration

A picks up a bag believing honestly that it is A's own identical bag. The act of taking occurred, but the dishonest intention required for theft may be absent. The outcome depends on the facts and the statutory ingredients.

## Conclusion

The essential elements of a crime are determined by the law defining that offence. Actus reus and mens rea are important concepts, but the statutory ingredients and any relevant defence determine liability.
