# BNS-1 — Syllabus-Wide Completeness Matrix

**Purpose:** Keep every examinable topic and its supporting material within the canonical subject folder: Semester-01/BNS-1/. Subfolders organise the material; the subject dashboard and this matrix link the parts together.

**Important:** A file existing does not mean its topic is complete. The status labels distinguish starter material from a fully reviewed exam-ready topic.

## Status key

- **Not started:** no meaningful lesson yet.
- **Starter:** a topic map or outline exists.
- **Expanded draft:** detailed explanation exists, but statutory/case coverage and exam review remain pending.
- **Verified:** source and legal review are complete for the stated scope.
- **Approved for main:** final diff reviewed and the owner explicitly authorises the PR merge.

No topic is labelled fully verified or approved merely because a draft exists.

## B. Required exam material for every major topic

Each topic should ultimately have:
- plain-English explanation from basic to exam level;
- exact BNS provisions, ingredients, explanations, exceptions and punishment provisions;
- at least one accurate hypothetical illustration;
- relevant verified case law where it materially helps;
- a full-length exam answer;
- short notes and comparison tables;
- original problem questions and answer keys;
- predicted questions labelled as predicted;
- verified PYQs only after the actual paper has been inspected;
- cross-links to the section finder and master index;
- source and review date.

## D. Release checklist

A topic may be labelled **Verified** only after:
- [ ] it is mapped to the applicable official CCSU syllabus;
- [ ] BNS provisions and amendments have been checked;
- [ ] ingredients, exceptions and penalties have been checked;
- [ ] cited judgments have been authenticated;
- [ ] model answers and practice questions have been reviewed;
- [ ] any PYQ claim is supported by the actual paper;
- [ ] links and navigation work;
- [ ] subject changelog is updated;
- [ ] review results are included in the PR.

The owner must authorise the specific PR before changes are merged into main. No process can guarantee perfection; the goal is traceable, repeatable error reduction.
