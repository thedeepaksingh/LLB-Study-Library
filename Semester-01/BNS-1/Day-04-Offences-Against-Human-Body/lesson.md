# DAY-04 — Detailed Lesson: Offences Against the Human Body

**Study identifier:** DAY-04  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Question status:** Practice questions are predicted, not verified PYQs.

## Learning outcomes

Understand the main human-body offences in the syllabus and learn how to compare offences by their statutory ingredients rather than by their everyday meanings.

## 1. Culpable homicide and murder

BNS Section 100 defines culpable homicide and Section 101 defines murder. The punishment provisions include Section 103 for murder and Section 105 for culpable homicide not amounting to murder. Always use the full statutory text, including clauses and exceptions.

### How to structure the distinction

| Point | What to analyse |
|---|---|
| Death | Was a person's death caused? |
| Mental element | What intention or knowledge is proved under the relevant statutory clause? |
| Circumstances | Do the circumstances fit a clause of culpable homicide or murder? |
| Exceptions | Is a statutory exception to murder relevant? |
| Conclusion | State the most appropriate legal classification on the facts |

**Core principle:** Murder is a more serious category within culpable homicide. The statutory clauses and exceptions, not a simple slogan, determine the classification.

## 2. Death caused by rash or negligent act

The BNS separately addresses causing death by negligence, including distinct circumstances addressed within the provision. Do not automatically classify every accidental death as criminal negligence or murder. Identify the required conduct, mental element or standard, causal link, and any fact-specific statutory condition.

Where a factual question mentions a road incident, also read the exact statutory sub-section and any particular qualification before discussing punishment.

## 3. Hurt and grievous hurt

Hurt and grievous hurt are distinct statutory categories. Grievous hurt is defined by the categories specified in the BNS; a serious-sounding injury is not automatically grievous hurt unless the legal definition is satisfied.

A good answer:
1. states the relevant definition;
2. lists the legally recognised categories;
3. applies the facts to the category;
4. distinguishes the basic offence from aggravated circumstances;
5. cites the correct punishment provision after verification.

## 4. Wrongful restraint and wrongful confinement

Wrongful restraint concerns obstructing a person so as to prevent movement in a direction where the law protects that freedom, subject to the provision. Wrongful confinement is more restrictive and concerns unlawfully preventing a person from proceeding beyond circumscribed limits.

| Wrongful restraint | Wrongful confinement |
|---|---|
| Obstruction of movement in a legally relevant direction | Unlawful restriction within limits that prevent movement beyond them |
| Scope may be limited to a direction or route | Involves confinement within boundaries |
| Apply the exact definition | Apply the exact definition |

## 5. Assault and criminal force

Do not treat assault and criminal force as interchangeable. The BNS defines force, criminal force and assault separately. Identify whether the facts involve the legally required gesture or preparation, actual use of force, intention/knowledge, consent and the other statutory ingredients. Confirm each definition from the official text.

## 6. Kidnapping and abduction

Kidnapping and abduction have distinct statutory definitions. Analyse who was moved or taken, from where, the person's age or capacity where relevant, consent, the means used, and the purpose where the statute makes it relevant. Do not assume the two terms have identical ingredients.

## Model answer outline: culpable homicide versus murder

**Introduction:** The BNS criminalises unlawful causing of death and distinguishes culpable homicide from murder according to statutory mental elements and circumstances.

**Provisions:** Section 100 defines culpable homicide; Section 101 defines murder. Check the exact wording and all relevant exceptions.

**Analysis:** Identify the mental element and statutory clause, apply the circumstances, and consider whether an exception may apply.

**Punishment:** After classifying the offence, consult the relevant punishment provision. Sections 103 and 105 are important starting points, but the applicable subsection must be read.

**Conclusion:** The legal classification depends on the complete statutory test and the facts, not solely on whether death occurred.
