# BNS Bare-Act Section-Wise Learning Guide
**CCSU LL.B. · Paper IV: Law of Crimes (BNS), K-1006 · Beginner edition**

This is the direct study guide for the statutory sections in the CCSU syllabus. The descriptions below explain each provision's role in plain English; they are **not a word-for-word replacement of the enacted Bare Act**. The official text controls. For every answer, cite the BNS number shown here rather than relying only on an old IPC number.

## 1. Foundations, liability and punishment

| BNS provision | Title / subject | What to learn for the exam |
|---|---|---|
| Section 2 | Definitions | Read definitions used throughout the Act, especially child, dishonestly, fraudulently, good faith, injury, illegal, and voluntarily. A statutory definition can be narrower than everyday English. |
| Section 3 | General explanations | Important rules of interpretation. Sub-section 3(5) is the joint-liability rule for a criminal act done by several persons in furtherance of their common intention. |
| Sections 4–13 | Punishments | Types of punishment, commutation, fractions, rigorous/simple imprisonment, fines and related rules. Do not confuse the definition of an offence with its punishment provision. |
| Sections 14–44 | General Exceptions | Excuses/justifications and private defence. Apply only the particular exception whose legal conditions fit the facts. |
| Sections 45–60 | Abetment | Instigation, conspiracy and intentional aid; liability and punishment in the circumstances set out in individual sections. |
| Section 61 | Criminal conspiracy | Agreement to do an illegal act, or a legal act by illegal means, subject to the statutory conditions. |
| Section 62 | Attempt | General attempt provision where the Act does not separately provide for that attempt. Use a specific attempt provision where one exists. |

### Core rule of interpretation
A heading helps locate a topic but does not replace the operative words. Read the definition, its qualifications, explanations, exceptions and linked punishment provision together. If a question states facts but not every legal element, identify what fact is still needed rather than assuming it.

## 2. General Exceptions — sections 14–44

| Section(s) | Subject | Study rule |
|---|---|---|
| 14–17 | Acts connected with legal duty/authority and mistakes of fact in specified situations | The belief and the facts that caused it must satisfy the statutory language. Mistake of fact is different from ignorance of law. |
| 18 | Accident | Requires the statutory conditions, including lawful conduct and proper care/caution; “I did not mean it” alone is insufficient. |
| 19 | Necessity / preventing greater harm | Assess good faith, the harm sought to be prevented, the harm risked and the statutory conditions. Not a general permission to break the law for convenience. |
| 20 | Child under seven | Absolute statutory immunity for the class covered by the section. |
| 21 | Child above seven and under twelve lacking sufficient maturity | Capacity is assessed according to the statutory test and the occasion; age alone does not answer every question in this band. |
| 22 | Unsoundness of mind | Focus on legally specified incapacity at the time of the act, not diagnosis alone. |
| 23–24 | Intoxication | Apply the different rules for intoxication without knowledge/against the person's will and for offences requiring a particular intent/knowledge. Voluntary intoxication is not a universal defence. |
| 25–30 | Consent-based exceptions and limits | Requirements vary by provision, including age, capacity, good faith, intended harm and the kind of offence. Consent is not a blanket defence. |
| 31–33 | Other specified exceptions | Read the actual section before applying; the chapter includes narrowly framed statutory situations, not an all-purpose equity defence. |
| 34–44 | Right of private defence | Defending body/property subject to the law's conditions, scope, duration and limits. It is not revenge. |

**Important:** This table groups nearby exceptions for navigation. In a problem, quote the exact section and test its conditions rather than citing the full range as though each section were identical.

## 3. Inchoate offences: sections 45–62

| Section(s) | Subject | Key legal question |
|---|---|---|
| 45–48 | Abetment and cross-border abetment situations | Did the accused instigate, intentionally aid, or participate in a legally sufficient abetting mode? Is the cross-border condition relevant? |
| 49–54 | Consequences and attribution when an offence is committed or a different act/result follows | What exactly was abetted, what was actually done, and which statutory attribution rule applies? |
| 55–57 | Abetment in particular offence or group circumstances | Check the offence's punishment class and the specific circumstance stated in the relevant section. |
| 58–60 | Concealment of a criminal design in specified cases | What knowledge/design existed, what concealment/omission occurred, and was there a legal duty or statutory condition? |
| 61 | Criminal conspiracy | Prove agreement and the statutory condition about the object and, where required, an act in pursuance of the agreement. |
| 62 | Attempt under the general provision | Intention to commit a particular offence plus conduct beyond preparation and sufficiently proximate to commission; confirm there is no special attempt section. |

**Exam warning:** BNS section 109 specifically concerns attempt to murder. Do not replace a special attempt provision with section 62 merely because the word “attempt” appears in the question.

## 4. Women and children — sections 63–99

| BNS section(s) | Subject | What must be checked |
|---|---|---|
| 63 | Definition of rape | The act and circumstances specified by the statutory definition; consent and statutory age limits. |
| 64–71 | Punishments and aggravated/repeat rape circumstances | Identify the correct punishment tier, victim's age, group involvement or prior conviction/other aggravating condition as relevant. |
| 72–73 | Disclosure/publication restrictions | The particular protected identity or proceeding, the act of disclosure/publication and statutory exceptions/permission rules. |
| 74 | Assault/criminal force to woman with intent to outrage modesty | Conduct, use of assault/criminal force and the specified intent. |
| 75 | Sexual harassment | Match the conduct to one of the statutory categories; do not use the label as a substitute for proof. |
| 76 | Assault or criminal force with intent to disrobe | Statutory intent and conduct; distinguish from sexual harassment and other offences. |
| 77 | Voyeurism | The statutorily defined viewing/capture/dissemination circumstances and the consent/expectation conditions. |
| 78 | Stalking | The defined following/contact/monitoring conduct and the statutory exceptions. |
| 79 | Words, gestures or acts intended to insult the modesty of a woman | The act and required intention. |
| 80 | Dowry death | Death circumstances, statutory time period, and cruelty/harassment for or in connection with dowry “soon before” death. |
| 81–84 | Other marriage-related offences specified in these sections | Identify the exact prohibited marriage/cohabitation/enticement/retention conduct and any knowledge/intent condition. |
| 85–86 | Cruelty by husband/relative and definition of cruelty | The relationship, conduct and one of the statutory cruelty categories. |
| 87 | Kidnapping/abduction of woman to compel marriage, etc. | Kidnapping/abduction, purpose/intent and the target act specified by the section. |
| 88–92 | Causing miscarriage and offences concerning unborn child | Consent, intention, stage/circumstance and the distinct consequences specified in the particular section. |
| 93–94 | Exposure/abandonment and concealment of birth | Age/status of child, relevant act, intention/knowledge and statutory conditions. |
| 95–99 | Using, procuring, kidnapping/abducting, selling or buying a child in specified circumstances | Child's age, prohibited purpose, accused's conduct and the exact offence definition. |

### Rape and sexual-offence answers
Give the statutory definition first; then assess age, consent and each aggravating circumstance. Distinguish the charged offence from related offences. Avoid importing general statements from older cases without checking current statutory wording and constitutional law.

## 5. Offences affecting the human body — sections 100–146

| BNS section(s) | Title / subject | Essential learning point |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | Culpable homicide | Death caused with the intention/knowledge described by this section. |
| 101 | Murder | Culpable homicide meeting the listed murder clauses, subject to statutory exceptions. |
| 102 | Culpable homicide by causing death of a person other than the intended person | Transferred consequence rules in the statutory circumstances; analyse the intended act and the person who actually died. |
| 103–104 | Punishment for murder; murder by life-convict | Use exact punishment provision and relevant sub-section. Section 103 includes a special group-murder provision for specified grounds; do not omit the statutory facts required. |
| 105 | Punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murder | Distinguish the applicable intention/knowledge category and then use the punishment tier in the text. |
| 106 | Causing death by negligence | Section 106(1) covers death by rash/negligent act not amounting to culpable homicide and gives a separate rule for registered medical practitioners performing a medical procedure. Section 106(2) was excluded from commencement and is not in force as at 20 October 2026 based on the latest checked materials. |
| 107–108 | Abetment of suicide in specified cases | Identify victim's category under section 107 or the general rule under section 108, plus the required abetting conduct. |
| 109–110 | Attempt to murder; attempt to commit culpable homicide | Distinguish intention/knowledge that would make the completed offence murder or culpable homicide, then apply the specific attempt provision. |
| 111–113 | Organised crime, petty organised crime, terrorist act | These are separate and specialised provisions; do not use them without establishing all their statutory definitions, thresholds and contextual conditions. They are not a substitute for analysing the syllabus's foundational offences. |
| 114–115 | Hurt; voluntarily causing hurt | Section 114 defines hurt. Section 115 requires voluntary causation as described by the Act. |
| 116–117 | Grievous hurt; voluntarily causing grievous hurt | Section 116 lists recognised categories of grievous hurt. Prove a listed category, not just that an injury seems serious. |
| 118–125 | Aggravated hurt/offences involving weapons, specific purposes or dangerous acts | Match the weapon, means, purpose, victim relationship/status and result to the exact sub-section. Do not assume all injuries use section 115/117. |
| 126–127 | Wrongful restraint; wrongful confinement | Restraint obstructs a protected direction; confinement prevents movement beyond a circumscribed limit. |
| 128–131 | Force, criminal force, assault and punishment-related provision(s) | Separate actual force/criminal force from assault by gesture or preparation causing apprehension, as the definitions specify. |
| 132–136 | Specified assaults/criminal force against public servants or in particular circumstances, and related provisions | Confirm public-servant status, lawful duty and the exact aggravating circumstances; do not infer the offence from job title alone. |
| 137–138 | Kidnapping; abduction | Kidnapping and abduction have distinct elements. For kidnapping from lawful guardianship, age, custody and guardian consent matter; abduction focuses on force or deceitful inducement to move a person. |
| 139–146 | Related kidnapping/abduction, trafficking, forced labour and other offences within the chapter | Select the exact provision by the accused's act and the statutory purpose, status of person, coercion/exploitation and other required elements. |

### The homicide ladder
1. Establish the death and causation.
2. Identify the intention or knowledge actually proved.
3. Compare section 100 and the clauses in section 101.
4. Check the exceptions to murder.
5. Choose the relevant punishment provision (103–106 or other applicable provision).
6. Explain why competing classifications do or do not fit.

Do not state punishments from memory when exact sub-section, age, prior conviction, group circumstance or statutory amendment may change the result.

## 6. State/public tranquillity — sections 147–158 and 189–194

| BNS section(s) | Subject | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| 147–151 | Waging war / related offences against the State and covered persons | Focus on the exact object, conduct and statutory conditions. |
| 152 | Acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India | Read the provision itself. It is not a verbatim re-enactment of IPC section 124A; “sedition is now section 152” is an inadequate legal analysis. |
| 153–158 | Other offences against the State, including specified assistance/concealment/escape-related conduct | Distinguish each defined offence and any required intent/knowledge. |
| 189 | Unlawful assembly | Statutory minimum of five persons and one of the listed common objects. |
| 190 | Liability by reason of membership of unlawful assembly | Link the offence to prosecution of common object, or members' knowledge that it was likely, under the exact section. |
| 191 | Rioting | Unlawful assembly plus force/violence in prosecution of the common object, as provided. |
| 192–193 | Provocation/landholder or related specified public-tranquillity offences | Use exact elements of the charge; do not assume all crowd disorder is rioting. |
| 194 | Affray | Fighting between two or more persons in a public place that disturbs public peace, subject to the section's language. |
| 195–197 | Other public-tranquillity/identity-related offences in this chapter | Read the individual definition and statutory conditions before classifying facts. |

## 7. Property offences — sections 303–334

| BNS section | Title | Main elements to write |
|---|---|---|
| 303 | Theft | Dishonest taking/movement of movable property from another's possession without consent, under the statutory definition. |
| 304 | Snatching | The specific sudden/forcible taking from a person or their possession covered by the section. |
| 305–307 | Aggravated/specially situated theft and related provisions | Identify the location/property/person and exact aggravating condition covered by the section. |
| 308 | Extortion | Intentional fear of injury plus dishonest inducement to deliver property/valuable security or a covered document. |
| 309 | Robbery | Theft or extortion plus the specified violence, attempted violence, restraint or fear-of-instant-harm connection. |
| 310 | Dacoity | Robbery/attempt by five or more persons conjointly, in the statutory circumstances. |
| 311–313 | Aggravated robbery/dacoity and related gang/thug provisions | Determine whether a weapon, attempt to cause death/grievous hurt, gang association or other specified circumstance is present. |
| 314 | Dishonest misappropriation of property | Dishonest misappropriation/conversion of movable property to own use. Initial possession may be innocent. |
| 315 | Misappropriation of property possessed by deceased person at the time of death | Analyse the statutory custody/time/knowledge requirements. |
| 316 | Criminal breach of trust | Entrustment/dominion + dishonest misappropriation/conversion/use/disposal contrary to law or governing direction/contract. |
| 317 | Stolen property | Establish the statutory status of property and the accused's knowledge/reason to believe, plus the act charged. |
| 318 | Cheating | Deception + required fraudulent/dishonest inducement and the consequence covered by the particular clause. |
| 319 | Cheating by personation | Deception by pretending to be another or substituting one person for another in the statutory sense. |
| 320–334 | Fraudulent dealings, mischief, trespass and other property-related offences | Identify the correct offence by the act, property interest, intention/knowledge and special statutory circumstances; these sections must not be treated as one generic “property offence”. |

### Property-offence test
- **Theft:** Was property moved dishonestly without consent?
- **Extortion:** Was fear used to induce delivery?
- **Robbery:** Is the statutory violence/restraint/instant-fear element linked to the taking?
- **Dacoity:** Are five or more persons conjointly involved?
- **Misappropriation:** When did dishonest conversion arise?
- **Breach of trust:** Was there entrustment/dominion before dishonest dealing?
- **Cheating:** Was the victim deceived and induced in the legally required way, particularly at the relevant time?
- **Personation:** Was a false identity used as specified?

## 9. Commencement and current-law caution
- BNS provisions generally came into force on **1 July 2024**.
- **Section 106(2) was excluded from the commencement notification** and remains not in force at the date this guide was checked (20 October 2026). Section 106(1) is distinct and in force.
- A statute being printed in the Act is not alone proof that a provision has commenced. Read commencement notifications where relevant.
- Transitional cases can involve the repeal-and-savings rule and the date of conduct. A case committed before the applicable commencement date may engage prior law depending on the statutory savings and constitutional principles.

## 10. How to cite a bare-act provision in a law exam
Good: “BNS section 303 requires dishonest taking/movement of movable property without the possessor's consent. On these facts, the disputed ingredient is dishonest intention.”

Weak: “Theft is under section 378” (that is the old IPC numbering) or “BNS says it is theft because the thing was taken.”

For long answers, mention both the current BNS section and a relevant old case only after explaining the legal principle for which the case is cited.
