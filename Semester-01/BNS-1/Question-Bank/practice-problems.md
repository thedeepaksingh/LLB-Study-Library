# BNS-1 — Original Practice Problems

**Classification:** PRACTICE — original hypotheticals written for learning. They are not previous-year questions.

For every answer, identify the issue, applicable BNS provision, ingredients, relevant defence, application to facts and conclusion. Where facts are insufficient, state what additional facts are needed rather than guessing.

## PRACTICE-BNS1-001 — Mistaken property

A leaves a library carrying an umbrella that looks exactly like their own, honestly believing it belongs to them. The true owner later complains.

**Questions:** What facts matter to dishonesty and consent? Is the physical act of taking alone sufficient to establish theft?

## PRACTICE-BNS1-002 — Preparation or attempt

A intends to steal a watch, obtains tools and goes to the building. A is arrested before touching the door.

**Questions:** Which stage is clearly established? What further conduct might be relevant to attempt? Explain why the result depends on the statutory offence and facts.

## PRACTICE-BNS1-003 — Group participation

A, B and C argue with D. During the encounter, A strikes D while B and C stand nearby; later evidence suggests B had earlier agreed to assist but C was attempting to stop the conflict.

**Questions:** What facts matter to common intention under Section 3(5)? Is presence enough? What distinctions should be made between the roles of A, B and C?

## PRACTICE-BNS1-004 — Unlawful assembly

Five or more people gather and share an object that may fit a category specified by the unlawful-assembly provision. During the incident, one member commits an offence.

**Questions:** What statutory facts must be proved for unlawful assembly? What additional requirements govern liability of the other members under Section 190?

## PRACTICE-BNS1-005 — Abetment

A repeatedly encourages B to commit an offence. B later carries it out.

**Questions:** Which statutory mode of abetment might be relevant? What must be proved about A's conduct and mental state? What further facts are required?

## PRACTICE-BNS1-006 — Private defence

B approaches A during a heated argument and threatens an immediate unlawful attack. A responds with force after the threat has ended.

**Questions:** What facts matter to the beginning and end of the right of private defence? Why is retaliation different from defensive action?

## PRACTICE-BNS1-007 — Death classification

During a confrontation, A intentionally strikes B with an object, and B later dies. The facts do not state the precise manner of the blow or A's knowledge of the likely consequence.

**Questions:** What further facts are needed to distinguish culpable homicide and murder? Explain the role of statutory clauses and exceptions.

## PRACTICE-BNS1-008 — Entrusted property

A is entrusted with property to use for a specified purpose, but A dishonestly converts it to personal use.

**Questions:** What is the significance of entrustment or dominion? What facts distinguish criminal breach of trust from cheating or another property offence?

## PRACTICE-BNS1-009 — Deception

A makes a false representation to B to obtain delivery of property. The facts do not state when A formed the dishonest intention.

**Questions:** Why might the timing and nature of deception matter? What elements must be checked under the cheating provision?

## PRACTICE-BNS1-010 — Restriction of movement

A blocks B from proceeding along a road. In another incident, A locks B in a room and prevents B from leaving.

**Questions:** Distinguish wrongful restraint from wrongful confinement and identify the facts that satisfy each definition.

## Evaluation checklist

- [ ] Correct issue identified.
- [ ] Statutory provision checked.
- [ ] Each ingredient analysed separately.
- [ ] Alternative offences considered where relevant.
- [ ] No fact or case law invented.
- [ ] Conclusion reflects uncertainty where facts are incomplete.
