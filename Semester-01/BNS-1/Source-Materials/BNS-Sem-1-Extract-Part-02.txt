   2. do a legal act by illegal means.

61.1 Essential Ingredients
1. Two or More Persons
A conspiracy requires at least:

       Two persons

A person cannot conspire with himself.




2. Agreement
There must be an agreement between the parties.

The essence of conspiracy is:

       Agreement

PART C – ATTEMPT

SECTION 62 – PUNISHMENT FOR
ATTEMPTING TO COMMIT OFFENCES
PUNISHABLE WITH IMPRISONMENT FOR
LIFE OR OTHER IMPRISONMENT
BNS Section: 62
Corresponding IPC: §511

🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
Attempt is the stage between:

      Preparation
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 85 / 748>
and

      Commission of the offence




62.1 Stages of Crime
This is a fundamental criminal-law concept.

Stage 1 — Intention
The person forms the intention to commit an offence.

Stage 2 — Preparation
The person makes arrangements to commit the offence.

Stage 3 — Attempt
The person performs an act towards the commission of the offence, going beyond mere
preparation.

Stage 4 — Commission
The offence is completed.

⭐ Formula
      Intention → Preparation → Attempt → Commission




62.2 Intention
Intention alone is generally not punishable.

Example
A thinks about stealing B's car.

No offence of theft has necessarily been committed merely because A formed the thought.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 86 / 748>
62.3 Preparation
Preparation consists of arranging the means necessary for committing the offence.

Example
A plans to rob a bank and purchases a mask and gloves.

Generally, this remains preparation.




62.4 Attempt
Attempt begins when the person performs an act sufficiently proximate to the commission of the
offence.

Example
A enters the bank with the weapon and begins demanding money.

The conduct may amount to attempt depending upon the facts.




62.5 Commission
The offence is completed when all the essential ingredients of the substantive offence are
fulfilled.

PART A – SEXUAL OFFENCES

SECTION 63 – RAPE
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 95 / 748>
BNS Section: 63
Corresponding IPC: §375

🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
Section 63 defines the offence of rape.

A man commits rape if he engages in any of the acts specified by the provision against a
woman under the circumstances specified in Section 63.

The provision covers four broad categories of sexual acts:

   1. Penetration of the penis into the vagina, mouth, urethra or anus;
   2. Insertion of an object or body part, other than the penis, into the vagina, urethra or anus;
   3. Manipulation of any part of the body causing penetration into the vagina, urethra, anus
      or other specified body parts; or
   4. Applying the mouth to the vagina, anus or urethra,

when the act occurs under one of the legally specified circumstances.

The circumstances include:

   ●   Against her will;
   ●   Without her consent;
   ●   With consent obtained by putting her or someone she is interested in in fear of death or
       hurt;
   ●   With consent where the man knows he is not her husband but she consents because
       she believes he is another man to whom she is lawfully married;
   ●   With consent where, because of unsoundness of mind or intoxication, she cannot
       understand the nature and consequence of the act;
   ●   With or without consent where she is under 18 years of age.




1. CONSENT
Consent is one of the most important aspects of Section 63.

The law distinguishes between:

Consent

and

Submission

A mere absence of physical resistance should not automatically be treated as consent.

⭐ Exam point
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 96 / 748>
Consent must be understood in the context of the statutory definition and surrounding
circumstances.




2. AGE OF THE WOMAN
For the statutory definition of rape:

      Sexual acts with a girl below 18 years fall within the statutory rape provision
      irrespective of consent.

Therefore:

Below 18 years → Consent does not make the sexual act lawful under the statutory
rape provision.




3. MEDICAL PROCEDURE AND CONSENT
Section 63 also contains an important exception concerning a medical procedure or
intervention.

A medical procedure/intervention does not become rape merely because it involves penetration
where the statutory conditions of the exception are satisfied.




4. MARITAL EXCEPTION
BNS retains the statutory marital exception in its rape provision, subject to the specific wording
and age-related qualification contained in the provision.

However, sexual intercourse by a husband with his wife during separation, where the wife
is living separately, is separately addressed under Section 67.

This distinction is important.

SECTION 100 – CULPABLE HOMICIDE
BNS Section: 100
Corresponding IPC: §299

🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
A person commits culpable homicide when he causes the death of a person by:

   1. Intending to cause death; or
   2. Intending to cause such bodily injury as is likely to cause death; or
   3. Knowing that the act is likely to cause death.

⭐ Formula
       Death + intention/knowledge + statutory circumstances = Culpable homicide




1. Essential Ingredients
For culpable homicide, establish:

1. Death
A human being must have died.

2. Causal connection
The accused's act must have caused the death.

3. Required mental element
There must be:

   ●    intention to cause death; OR
   ●    intention to cause bodily injury likely to cause death; OR
   ●    knowledge that the act is likely to cause death.




2. Intention vs Knowledge
This distinction is extremely important.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 128 / 748>
Intention
The person desires or consciously aims at the consequence.

Knowledge
The person may not desire the consequence but knows it is likely to occur.

Example
A deliberately shoots B intending to kill him.

→ Intention

A performs an extremely dangerous act knowing that death is likely, although death is not his
desired outcome.

→ Knowledge




⚖️ CASE LAW
State of A.P. v. Rayavarapu Punnayya
One of the most important cases for:

      Culpable homicide vs murder

The Supreme Court described culpable homicide as the genus and murder as its species.

⭐ Memorise
      Every murder is culpable homicide, but every culpable homicide is not
      murder.




SECTION 101 – MURDER
BNS Section: 101
Corresponding IPC: §300

🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 129 / 748>
Culpable homicide becomes murder where the circumstances specified in Section 101 are
satisfied.

Broadly, culpable homicide is murder where the act causing death falls within the statutory
categories concerning:

   1. Intention to cause death;
   2. Intention to cause bodily injury known to be likely to cause death of that particular
      person;
   3. Intention to cause bodily injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death;
      or
   4. Knowledge that the act is so imminently dangerous that it must, in all probability, cause
      death or such bodily injury likely to cause death, and the act is done without justification
      for incurring the risk.




1. FOUR CLAUSES OF MURDER
Clause 1 — Intention to Cause Death
If a person intentionally causes death, the case falls within murder.

Example
A deliberately shoots B with the intention of killing him.




Clause 2 — Particular Knowledge
The accused intentionally causes bodily injury knowing that the particular victim has a special
condition making death likely.

Example
A knows B suffers from a serious heart condition.

A intentionally inflicts a bodily injury knowing that because of B's condition it is likely to cause
death.




Clause 3 — Injury Sufficient in Ordinary Course of Nature
to Cause Death
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 130 / 748>
This is one of the most important murder principles.

The accused:

   ●    intends to cause a particular bodily injury;
   ●    actually causes that injury; and
   ●    the injury is sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death.

⚖️ CASE
Virsa Singh v. State of Punjab

This is a must-remember case.

The Supreme Court laid down the classic test for the third clause of murder.

⭐ Exam principle
The prosecution must establish:

   1.   A bodily injury was present;
   2.   The nature of the injury was proved;
   3.   The accused intended to inflict that particular injury; and
   4.   The injury was sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death.




Clause 4 — Imminently Dangerous Act
An act is murder where:

   ●    it is imminently dangerous;
   ●    it must, in all probability, cause death or such bodily injury as is likely to cause death;
   ●    the accused knows the risk; and
   ●    the act is committed without justification for taking that risk.




SECTION 101 — EXCEPTIONS TO
MURDER
🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
Culpable homicide may not amount to murder if one of the statutory exceptions applies.

The five traditional exceptions are:
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 131 / 748>
Exception 1
Grave and sudden provocation

Exception 2
Exceeding the right of private defence

Exception 3
Public servant exceeding powers

Exception 4
Sudden fight

Exception 5
Consent




EXCEPTION 1 – GRAVE AND SUDDEN
PROVOCATION
A person may lose self-control because of:

    ●    grave;
    ●    sudden provocation;

and cause the death of the person who gave the provocation, subject to the statutory conditions.

⚠️ Important
The provocation must be:

        Grave + Sudden

A trivial insult will not automatically qualify.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 132 / 748>
EXCEPTION 2 – EXCESSIVE PRIVATE
DEFENCE
Where a person:

   ●    acts in good faith;
   ●    exercises private defence;
   ●    exceeds the legal limits of that right;
   ●    without premeditation;
   ●    and without intending to cause more harm than necessary,

the offence may fall within culpable homicide rather than murder, subject to the statutory
conditions.




EXCEPTION 3 – PUBLIC SERVANT
A public servant, or person assisting a public servant, may fall within this exception where:

   ●    the act is done while exercising powers;
   ●    he exceeds those powers;
   ●    he acts in good faith;
   ●    and without ill-will toward the person killed.




EXCEPTION 4 – SUDDEN FIGHT
This exception applies where death occurs during:

   ●    a sudden fight;
   ●    in the heat of passion;
   ●    without premeditation;
   ●    without undue advantage or cruelty.

⭐ Key elements
       Sudden fight + heat of passion + no premeditation + no undue advantage
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 133 / 748>
EXCEPTION 5 – CONSENT
Where a person who is:

   ●    above 18 years of age,

suffers death with his own consent, the case may fall within this exception subject to statutory
conditions.

SECTION 105 – PUNISHMENT FOR

This section has two important categories.

   ●    with intention to cause death; or
   ●    with intention to cause bodily injury likely to cause death;

   ●    with knowledge that it is likely to cause death;
   ●    but without intention to cause death or such bodily injury,

SECTION 109 – ATTEMPT TO MURDER
BNS Section: 109
Corresponding IPC: §307

🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
A person commits attempt to murder where he:

   ●    does an act;
   ●    with such intention or knowledge; and
   ●    under such circumstances;

that if death were caused, the act would amount to murder.

⭐ Key point
Actual death is NOT required.




Example
A shoots at B intending to kill him.

B survives.

A may still be guilty of attempt to murder.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 140 / 748>
SECTION 109(2) – SERIOUS ATTEMPT TO
MURDER
Where a person already undergoing imprisonment for life commits an act constituting attempt to
murder and causes hurt, the punishment can be extremely severe, including:

      Death or imprisonment for life for the remainder of natural life.




⚖️ CASE LAW
State of Maharashtra v. Balram Bama Patil
The Supreme Court explained that actual injury is not always necessary to establish attempt to
murder; the intention and circumstances of the act are crucial.
