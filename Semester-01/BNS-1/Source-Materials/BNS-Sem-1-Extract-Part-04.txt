SECTION 189 – UNLAWFUL ASSEMBLY

🔴🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
This is the foundation of the chapter.

IPC equivalent
Primarily IPC §141, with the subsequent IPC unlawful-assembly provisions incorporated into the
BNS framework.




Definition
An assembly of five or more persons becomes an unlawful assembly if the common object of
the persons falls within any of the categories specified by the section.




⭐ FIVE OR MORE PERSONS
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 232 / 748>
This is the first thing you should remember.

Minimum:
5 persons

So:

      ●   2 persons → cannot constitute unlawful assembly under §189.
      ●   4 persons → cannot constitute unlawful assembly under §189.
      ●   5 or more → may constitute unlawful assembly, provided the common-object
          requirement is satisfied.

⚠️ Five people alone are NOT enough.
There must also be a legally prohibited common object.




FIVE COMMON OBJECTS
An assembly becomes unlawful if its common object is one of the statutory objects, including:

1. To overawe Government/public authority
To overawe by criminal force or show of criminal force:

      ●   Central Government;
      ●   State Government;
      ●   Parliament;
      ●   State Legislature;
      ●   public servant in exercise of lawful powers.




2. To resist execution of law
To resist the execution of any law or legal process.

Example
Five persons gather and use force to prevent police officers from lawfully executing a court
order.




3. To commit an offence
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 233 / 748>
To commit:

   ●   mischief;
   ●   criminal trespass; or
   ●   any other offence.




4. To take property by force
To take possession of property or obtain a right over property by:

   ●   criminal force;
   ●   show of criminal force.




5. To compel someone
To compel a person to do something he is not legally bound to do, or prevent him from doing
something he is legally entitled to do.




🧠 EASY MEMORY
Remember the five purposes:

GOVERNMENT

LAW

OFFENCE

PROPERTY

COMPULSION
If five or more persons assemble with one of these unlawful common objects, §189 becomes
relevant.




⭐ COMMON OBJECT
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 234 / 748>
The term common object is extremely important.

It means the object shared by the members of the unlawful assembly.

Important:
All members do not necessarily need to perform the same physical act.

What matters is whether they shared the relevant common object or knowingly became
members of the assembly having that object.

SECTION 190 – LIABILITY OF MEMBERS
OF UNLAWFUL ASSEMBLY
🔴🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §149

This is one of the most important provisions in the entire BNS for examination.




Basic principle
If an offence is committed by any member of an unlawful assembly:

   ●     in prosecution of the common object of that assembly; or
   ●     is an offence which the members knew to be likely to be committed in prosecution of
         that object,

then:

        Every person who, at the time of committing that offence, is a member of that
        assembly is guilty of that offence.




⭐ Why is §190 important?
Because a person does not necessarily have to:

   ●     personally strike the victim;
   ●     personally carry the weapon;
   ●     personally cause the injury.

If the statutory conditions are established, membership in the unlawful assembly can result in
liability.




Example
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 236 / 748>
Five persons go to attack X.

A carries a knife.

B, C, D and E surround X.

A stabs X.

If the stabbing was committed in prosecution of the common object, or was an offence the
members knew was likely to be committed, §190 may make the other members liable as
well, depending on the facts.




⚠️ IMPORTANT
Mere presence is not automatically enough.
The prosecution must establish that the accused was a member of the unlawful assembly
and that the statutory common-object/knowledge requirements are satisfied.




⚖️🔴 CASE LAW – Lalji v State of U.P.
   MUST KNOW

The Supreme Court explained the principle of constructive liability under the old IPC §149.

Principle
Once the necessary ingredients of unlawful assembly and common object are established, each
member can be held liable for the offence committed in prosecution of that common object.




⚖️ CASE LAW – Masalti v State of U.P.
The Supreme Court recognised that in cases involving large unlawful assemblies, it is not
always necessary to prove that every individual member committed a specific physical act.

The focus is on:

   ●   membership;
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 237 / 748>
     ●    common object;
     ●    participation in the unlawful assembly;
     ●    statutory requirements for constructive liability.




SECTION 191 – RIOTING
🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §§146–148

An unlawful assembly becomes a riot when:

         force or violence is used by the unlawful assembly, or by any member
         thereof, in prosecution of the common object.




Formula
UNLAWFUL ASSEMBLY
5+ persons + unlawful common object

⬇️
RIOTING
Unlawful assembly + force/violence




Example
Five people assemble with the common object of forcibly taking possession of property.

They begin attacking the occupants and damaging property.

The assembly may constitute rioting.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 238 / 748>
⭐ IMPORTANT
Every riot involves an unlawful assembly.
But:

Every unlawful assembly is NOT necessarily a riot.
Why?

Because an unlawful assembly can exist without actual force or violence.

2. MOST IMPORTANT CONCEPT –
CONSTRUCTIVE LIABILITY
BNS §190
This is a major examination concept.

Suppose:

A+B+C+D+E

form an unlawful assembly.

They share a common object.

A commits an offence while pursuing that object.

Under §190, the other members may also be held liable if the statutory conditions are satisfied.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 245 / 748>
⚠️ BUT REMEMBER
It is wrong to write:

        "Every member is automatically guilty of every offence committed by any member."

That is too broad.

Correct statement:

        Every member may be liable where the offence was committed in prosecution of
        the common object, or where the offence was one that the members knew was
        likely to be committed in prosecution of that object.




3. COMMON OBJECT CAN DEVELOP
Another important point:

A common object does not necessarily have to be formed after a long prior conspiracy.

Persons can assemble for one lawful purpose and subsequently develop an unlawful common
object.

Example
Five people initially gather for a lawful purpose.

During the gathering, they collectively decide to use force to attack X.

If the statutory ingredients are subsequently established, the assembly can become unlawful.

PART A – FALSE EVIDENCE

SECTION 227 – GIVING FALSE EVIDENCE

🔴🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 296 / 748>
IPC §191

This is one of the most important definitions in the entire chapter.

A person gives false evidence when:

   1. he is legally bound by an oath or by an express provision of law to state the truth; or
   2. he is required by law to make a declaration upon any subject;

and

   3. he makes a statement which is false;
   4. he either:
         ○ knows it to be false, or
         ○ believes it to be false, or
         ○ does not believe it to be true.




⭐ What is a "statement"?
A statement can concern:

   ●    facts;
   ●    opinions;
   ●    beliefs;
   ●    matters relevant to the proceeding,

depending upon the statutory circumstances.




Example
A witness is asked:

       "Did you see X at the place of occurrence?"

A knows that he did not see X but deliberately says:

       "Yes, I saw him."

A may be giving false evidence.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 297 / 748>
🔥 IMPORTANT
False evidence is not limited to statements made in a courtroom.

The provision can operate wherever the person is legally bound to state the truth under the
circumstances contemplated by the law.




SECTION 228 – FABRICATING FALSE
EVIDENCE

🔴🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §192

This is different from giving false evidence.

A person fabricates false evidence when he:

   ●   causes a circumstance to exist;
   ●   makes a false entry in a book/record/electronic record;
   ●   makes a document/electronic record containing a false statement;

with the intention that it may appear in evidence in a judicial proceeding or proceeding before a
public servant, arbitrator, etc., and that it may cause the person who relies upon it to form an
erroneous opinion on a material point.




Example
A wants to falsely implicate B.

A creates a fake electronic record showing that B transferred money to the victim.

A intends the record to be produced as evidence.

This may constitute fabrication of false evidence.

SECTION 229 – PUNISHMENT FOR FALSE
EVIDENCE
IPC equivalent
IPC §193
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 299 / 748>
The punishment depends upon whether the false evidence is given in:

   ●    a judicial proceeding; or
   ●    another situation contemplated by the provision.

General principle
False evidence is treated particularly seriously when it is given in a judicial proceeding,
because it can result in wrongful conviction or miscarriage of justice.




⭐ WHY IS FALSE EVIDENCE SERIOUS?
Because it can:

   ●    convict an innocent person;
   ●    acquit a guilty person;
   ●    distort judicial findings;
   ●    waste judicial resources;
   ●    undermine public confidence in courts.

PROCURE CONVICTION FOR CAPITAL
OFFENCE
🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §194

This provision applies where a person gives or fabricates false evidence intending to procure
the conviction of another person for an offence punishable with death.

Why more serious?
Because the fabricated/false evidence can potentially result in:

       wrongful execution of an innocent person.

Therefore, the law imposes an extremely severe punishment.
