SECTION 270 – PUBLIC NUISANCE
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 328 / 748>

A person is guilty of public nuisance when he:

      ●   does an act; or
      ●   is guilty of an illegal omission;

which causes:

      ●   common injury;
      ●   danger;
      ●   annoyance

to:

      ●   the public;
      ●   people generally who dwell or occupy property in the vicinity;

      ●   injury;
      ●   obstruction;
      ●   danger;
      ●   annoyance

to persons using a public right.

The section also makes clear that a common nuisance is not excused merely because it

⭐ Meaning of Public Nuisance
Public nuisance is essentially an interference with a right or convenience enjoyed by the
public generally, rather than merely an interference with one individual's private right.

Example
A person blocks a public road with construction material, preventing members of the public from
using it.

This may constitute public nuisance.

3. VERY IMPORTANT CONCEPT –
RASHNESS VS NEGLIGENCE
This chapter repeatedly uses:

      rash or negligent

You should understand the distinction.

Rashness
A person consciously takes a risk, knowing that his conduct may cause harmful consequences,
but proceeds anyway.

Negligence
A person fails to exercise the reasonable care expected in the circumstances.

Criminal negligence
Not every ordinary mistake is criminal negligence.

The conduct must satisfy the applicable criminal-law threshold.

🔥RELIGION"
   §299 IS NOT "ANY INSULT TO

This is a very important examination point.

The provision does not mean:

       Every offensive statement about a religion is automatically criminal.

The statutory requirement of:

       deliberate + malicious intention

is crucial.




Example 1
A accidentally makes an incorrect statement about a religious practice.

There is no deliberate or malicious intention.

Result
§299 is not automatically attracted.




Example 2
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 362 / 748>

The ingredients of §299 may be established.

I. THEFT
🔴 SECTION 303 – THEFT
🔄 IPC → BNS
IPC §378 → BNS §303(1) – Definition
 IPC §379 → BNS §303(2) – Punishment

Definition
A person commits theft when he:

   1.   Intends dishonestly to take movable property;
   2.   The property is in the possession of another person;
   3.   Takes it without that person's consent; and
   4.   Moves the property in order to take it.

This is the basic statutory structure of §303.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 379 / 748>
⭐ Five essential ingredients of theft
Remember:

        D-M-P-C-M

D – Dishonest intention
M – Movable property
P – Possession of another
C – Without consent
M – Moving the property

1. Dishonest intention
The intention must be to cause:

    ●   wrongful gain to one person; or
    ●   wrongful loss to another.

If there is no dishonest intention, there is no theft.

Example
A honestly believes that B's umbrella is his own and takes it.

There is no dishonest intention → ordinarily, no theft.




2. Property must be movable
The subject matter of theft must be movable property.

Land itself cannot be stolen.

However, something attached to the earth can become capable of being stolen once it is
severed.

Example
A cuts and removes B's tree.

Once severed, the wood becomes movable property and may become the subject of theft.




3. Property must be in possession of another
The important concept is possession, not necessarily ownership.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 380 / 748>
Example
A lends his watch to B.

B possesses the watch although A owns it.

If C dishonestly takes the watch from B's possession, C may commit theft.




4. Taking must be without consent
Consent may be:

   ●   express; or
   ●   implied.

If the person in possession freely consents to the taking, theft is not established.




5. There must be movement
The property must be moved in order to accomplish the taking.

Even a very small movement can be sufficient.

The BNS also treats removal of an obstacle or separation of property as movement for this
purpose.




⭐ Punishment under §303(2)
Ordinarily:

   ●   imprisonment up to 3 years, or
   ●   fine, or
   ●   both.

⚠️ Important BNS change
For a second or subsequent conviction, §303 provides:

   ●   rigorous imprisonment of not less than 1 year and up to 5 years, and
   ●   fine.

Special first-time low-value theft rule
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 381 / 748>
Where:

   ●     stolen property is worth less than ₹5,000;
   ●     it is the person's first conviction; and
   ●     the property/value is returned/restored,

the punishment is community service.

🔴 Exam Point: Community service is one of the notable changes introduced by BNS.

⚖️ Important case law – Theft
Pyare Lal Bhargava v State of Rajasthan
The Supreme Court dealt with the concept of taking movable property and possession in the
context of theft.

Exam principle
       Theft is primarily concerned with dishonest moving of movable property from
       another's possession without consent.
