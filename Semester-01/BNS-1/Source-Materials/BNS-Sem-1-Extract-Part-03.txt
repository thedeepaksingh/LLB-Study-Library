SECTION 147 – WAGING WAR AGAINST
GOVERNMENT OF INDIA
🔴 MUST STUDY
Meaning
A person commits this offence when he:

   ●   wages war against the Government of India;
   ●   attempts to wage such war; or
   ●   abets the waging of such war.

IPC equivalent
IPC §121

Punishment
The punishment is:

   ●   Death, or
   ●   Imprisonment for life, and
   ●   fine.

What does "waging war" mean?
Waging war does not necessarily mean a conventional war between two armies.

The essential question is whether the accused's conduct amounts to an attempt to challenge or
overthrow the authority of the Government by organised violent force.

Example
Suppose a large armed group organises itself, obtains weapons, attacks government
establishments and attempts to overthrow governmental authority.

This may constitute waging war against the Government of India.




⚖️Attack
     CASE LAW – State v Navjot Sandhu (Parliament
         Case)
The Supreme Court examined the concept of waging war against the Government of India.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 167 / 748>
Principle
The offence requires conduct of a serious nature directed against the authority of the
Government. Mere violence or an ordinary criminal act does not automatically amount to waging
war.




🔥 EXAM POINT
Waging war ≠ ordinary violence

A person attacking another individual because of a personal dispute does not ordinarily commit
waging war.

The offence concerns an attack directed against the Government/State authority with the
requisite character of organised or serious insurrectionary force.




SECTION 148 – CONSPIRACY TO COMMIT
OFFENCES PUNISHABLE BY §147
🔴 IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §121A

Offence
A person commits this offence when he conspires:

   1. to commit an offence punishable under §147; or
   2. to overawe the Central Government or a State Government by:
          ○ criminal force, or
          ○ show of criminal force.

Important point
No overt act is necessary.

The BNS expressly provides that, for this conspiracy offence, it is not necessary that any act
or illegal omission takes place in pursuance of the conspiracy.

   ●   imprisonment for life, or
   ●   imprisonment up to 10 years,
   ●   and fine.

SECTION 152 – ACT ENDANGERING
SOVEREIGNTY, UNITY AND INTEGRITY
OF INDIA

🔴🔴 EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
This is one of the most important BNS provisions for examination.

It is particularly important because it replaced the old IPC framework of sedition under §124A,
although it is not simply a renumbered sedition provision.




IPC → BNS
IPC §124A – Sedition

⬇️
BNS §152 – Act endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India

⚠️ Do not write: "Sedition has simply become BNS §152."
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 171 / 748>
That is an oversimplification.




What does §152 prohibit?
A person commits the offence when, purposely or knowingly, by:

      ●   words spoken;
      ●   words written;
      ●   signs;
      ●   visible representation;
      ●   electronic communication;
      ●   use of financial means; or
      ●   otherwise,

he:

      ●   excites or attempts to excite:
              ○ secession;
              ○ armed rebellion;
              ○ subversive activities;
      ●   encourages feelings of separatist activities;
      ●   endangers the sovereignty, unity or integrity of India; or
      ●   indulges in or commits any such act.

Punishment
      ●   imprisonment for life, or
      ●   imprisonment up to 7 years,
      ●   and fine.




🔥 MOST IMPORTANT EXPLANATION
Section 152 contains an important safeguard.

Mere lawful criticism of Government is NOT enough.
The Explanation provides that comments expressing disapprobation of Government
measures or administrative action, when made with a view to obtaining their alteration by
lawful means, do not constitute an offence under §152, provided they do not excite or attempt
to excite the prohibited activities.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 172 / 748>
Example 1 – Not ordinarily §152
A citizen says:

      "I strongly disagree with this government policy and want Parliament to change it."

This is criticism seeking lawful alteration.

It does not automatically constitute §152.




Example 2 – Potential §152
A person deliberately uses electronic communications to encourage an armed rebellion against
India and calls upon people to participate in it.

This may fall within §152.




Example 3 – Separatist activity
A person knowingly organises and financially supports activities intended to promote the
separation of a part of India from the Union.

This can attract §152, depending on the facts and proof of the statutory ingredients.

⚖️ IMPORTANT SEDITION CASE LAW
Although IPC §124A is repealed as an operative provision, its constitutional jurisprudence
remains important for understanding the distinction between legitimate criticism and
punishable anti-State conduct.


1. Kedar Nath Singh v State of Bihar
🔴 MUST KNOW
The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of IPC §124A but restricted its application.

Principle
Strong criticism of the Government is not by itself sedition.

There must be a connection with:

   ●   incitement to violence; or
   ●   intention/tendency to create public disorder through violent means.

Exam use
Use this case when discussing:

Freedom of speech vs offences against the State.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 174 / 748>
2. Balwant Singh v State of Punjab
The accused raised slogans such as slogans concerning Khalistan.

Principle
Mere casual slogans, without the required intention or tendency to create violence/public
disorder, were not sufficient to establish sedition in the circumstances of that case.

Exam use
Excellent case for explaining the difference between:

political expression and criminal anti-State activity.




3. S.G. Vombatkere v Union of India
The Supreme Court's 2022 order concerning IPC §124A resulted in a temporary restraint on the
registration of fresh sedition cases and continuation of proceedings under the provision while
the constitutional challenge was being considered.

Exam relevance
This demonstrates the constitutional controversy surrounding the old sedition provision.

⚠️ However, do not substitute this case for the statutory analysis of BNS §152.

SECTION 159 – ABETTING MUTINY OR
SEDUCING FROM DUTY
🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §131

This provision protects military discipline.

A person commits the offence if he:

     ●   abets the committing of mutiny by:
            ○ an officer,
            ○ soldier,
            ○ sailor, or
            ○ airman;

OR

     ●   attempts to seduce such person from his duty or allegiance.

Important concepts

Mutiny
Mutiny means collective or organised disobedience/rebellion by members of the armed forces
against lawful military authority.

Seducing from duty
It means attempting to influence a member of the armed forces so that he abandons or violates
his lawful military duty or allegiance.

Punishment
Imprisonment for life, or imprisonment up to 10 years, and fine.




Example
A person deliberately attempts to persuade soldiers of the Indian Army to abandon their lawful
orders and revolt against their superior military authorities.

The conduct may attract §159.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 186 / 748>
⭐ EXAM POINT
The offence can be committed even when mutiny does not ultimately occur.

Why?

Because §159 covers both:

   1. abetting mutiny, and
   2. attempting to seduce a member from duty or allegiance.




SECTION 160 – ABETMENT OF MUTINY, IF
MUTINY IS COMMITTED
IPC equivalent
IPC §132

This is closely related to §159.

The important distinction is:

§159
Abetment/attempt to seduce.

§160
Mutiny is actually committed in consequence of the abetment.




Ingredients
There must be:

   1. abetment of mutiny;
   2. by a member of the armed forces; and
   3. mutiny must actually be committed in consequence of that abetment.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 187 / 748>
Punishment
Death, or imprisonment for life, or imprisonment up to 10 years, and fine.

SECTION 163 – ABETTING DESERTION
🔴 IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §135

Desertion
Desertion means abandoning military service/duty without lawful authority, with the requisite
intention to permanently leave that service.

A person who abets desertion by a member of:

   ●   Army,
   ●   Navy, or
   ●   Air Force

commits the offence.

Punishment
Imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine, or both.




Example
A person knowingly assists a soldier in abandoning his military service and encourages him to
remain away from duty permanently.

The person may be prosecuted under §163.




⚠️LEAVE
    DESERTION ≠ ABSENCE WITHOUT

This distinction is important.

Desertion
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 190 / 748>
Generally involves an intention to abandon military service.

Absence without leave
A person may be absent without authorisation but may still intend to return to service.

Therefore:

Every desertion involves unauthorised absence, but every unauthorised absence does
not necessarily amount to desertion.




SECTION 164 – HARBOURING DESERTER
IPC equivalent
IPC §136

A person commits this offence when he:

   ●   knowingly harbours a deserter.

Who is a deserter?
A member of the:

   ●   Army,
   ●   Navy, or
   ●   Air Force

who has deserted.

Punishment
Imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine, or both.




Example
A soldier deserts his unit.

A civilian knowingly provides him a place to hide and helps him avoid military authorities.

The civilian may attract §164.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 191 / 748>
⭐ EXAM POINT
Knowledge is important.

The provision concerns knowingly harbouring the deserter.

Therefore, merely giving accommodation to someone without knowing that he is a deserter
does not automatically establish the offence.

SECTION 169 – CANDIDATE, ELECTORAL
RIGHT AND RELATED DEFINITIONS
🔴 IMPORTANT FOUNDATION SECTION
IPC equivalent
IPC §171A

This section defines important expressions used in the election-offence provisions.




1. Candidate
A candidate means a person who has been nominated as a candidate at an election.

It also covers a person who has been proposed as a candidate.




2. Electoral right
Electoral right means the right of a person:

   ●   to stand, or not to stand, as a candidate;
   ●   to withdraw or not withdraw from being a candidate; or
   ●   to vote or refrain from voting at an election.

🔴 This definition is important because several election offences interfere with these rights.

SECTION 170 – BRIBERY
🔴🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §171B

Bribery is committed when a person gives or accepts a gratification with the intention of
influencing the exercise of an electoral right.




What is "gratification"?
The concept is broader than simply giving cash.

It can include:

   ●   money;
   ●   valuable benefits;
   ●   advantages;
   ●   other forms of gratification.




Two sides of bribery
A. Giving gratification
A person gives or promises gratification:

   ●   to induce a person to exercise an electoral right in a particular manner; or
   ●   as a reward for exercising such right.


B. Accepting gratification
A person accepts gratification:

   ●   as a motive or reward for exercising an electoral right in a particular manner.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 201 / 748>
Example
Candidate A tells voter B:

      "Vote for me and I will give you ₹5,000."

If B accepts the gratification in connection with the exercise of the electoral right, the conduct
may constitute bribery.




⭐ IMPORTANT
Bribery does not necessarily require:

      "Vote for me."

It can also involve influencing a person to:

      refrain from voting

because the electoral right includes the right to vote or refrain from voting.




SECTION 171 – UNDUE INFLUENCE AT
ELECTIONS
🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
IPC equivalent
IPC §171C

Undue influence occurs when a person voluntarily interferes or attempts to interfere with the
free exercise of an electoral right.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 202 / 748>
What can constitute undue influence?
The provision covers interference through:

1. Threat
For example:

    ●    threat of injury;
    ●    threat to person;
    ●    threat to reputation;
    ●    threat to property.

2. Spiritual influence
The provision also recognises interference through spiritual/ religious influence in the
statutory sense.

3. Other interference
Any intentional interference with the free exercise of electoral rights can potentially fall within the
provision if its statutory ingredients are established.




Example
A local political leader tells villagers:

        "Anyone who votes against my chosen candidate will lose his employment."

This may amount to undue influence.




⚠️ IMPORTANT EXCEPTION
Mere declaration of public policy or exercise of legal rights is not
automatically undue influence.
For example:

        "If elected, I will introduce a new tax policy."

This is part of ordinary political/electoral campaigning and is not automatically undue influence.

SECTION 175 – FALSE STATEMENT IN

IPC equivalent
IPC §171G

A person commits this offence when he:

   ●   which he knows to be false;
   ●   concerning the personal character or conduct of a candidate;
   ●   with the intention of affecting the result of an election.




🔥 THREE INGREDIENTS
Remember:
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 206 / 748>
FALSE + CANDIDATE + ELECTION
There must be:

   1.   a statement;
   2.   knowledge that it is false;
   3.   statement concerning the personal character/conduct of a candidate;
   4.   intention to affect the election result.




Example
During an election campaign, A knowingly publishes a completely false allegation that candidate
B committed a serious act of dishonesty, solely to damage B's electoral prospects.

If the statutory ingredients are established, §175 may apply.




⚠️ IMPORTANT
The section does not criminalise every criticism of a candidate.

There must be the required:

   ●    falsity,
   ●    knowledge, and
   ●    intention relating to the election result.

The electoral right includes the right to refrain from voting.

A is offering gratification to influence B's electoral right.

Therefore, subject to the statutory ingredients being proved, the conduct falls within bribery
under BNS §170.




📝 ANOTHER PROBLEM
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 212 / 748>
A threatens B:

       "If you vote against my candidate, I will damage your business."

There is no payment or other gratification.

Answer
The facts point toward undue influence under §171, rather than bribery.




📝 THIRD PROBLEM
A knowingly publishes a false allegation about Candidate B's personal conduct immediately
before voting, intending to damage B's electoral prospects.

Answer
The facts may attract §175, provided the statutory requirements of:

   ●    false statement,
   ●    knowledge of falsity,
   ●    personal character/conduct of candidate, and
   ●    intention to affect election result

are established.

SECTION 178 – COUNTERFEITING COIN,
CURRENCY-NOTES OR BANK-NOTES
🔴🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
This provision deals with the actual act of counterfeiting.

Meaning of counterfeiting
Counterfeiting means making something resemble a genuine coin, currency-note or bank-note
with the intention that it may be passed off as genuine.




What can be counterfeited?
The provision covers:

   ●    coins;
   ●    currency-notes;
   ●    bank-notes.

Example
A person manufactures a fake ₹500 note designed to look sufficiently like a genuine currency
note and intends that people accept it as genuine.

That conduct may constitute counterfeiting.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 216 / 748>
⭐ IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
Counterfeiting
Making the fake item.

Using counterfeit
Passing/using the fake item as genuine.

These are separate stages and offences.




SECTION 179 – USING COUNTERFEIT
COIN, CURRENCY-NOTE OR BANK-NOTE
AS GENUINE
🔴 VERY IMPORTANT
This provision deals with a person who:

   ●   sells;
   ●   buys;
   ●   receives;
   ●   traffics in; or
   ●   uses

counterfeit:

   ●   coin;
   ●   currency-note; or
   ●   bank-note,

as genuine.




Knowledge is critical
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 217 / 748>
The accused must have the requisite knowledge or reason to believe that the item is
counterfeit.

Example
A receives a fake ₹500 note.

At the time of receiving it, A genuinely believes it is genuine.

Later A discovers that it is counterfeit.

If A then deliberately uses it as genuine, §179 may become relevant.




🔥 EXAM POINT
Mere possession of counterfeit currency is not automatically the same as using it as
genuine.

You must examine:

    1. possession;
    2. knowledge/reason to believe;
    3. intention/use as genuine.




SECTION 180 – POSSESSION OF
COUNTERFEIT COIN, CURRENCY-NOTE
OR BANK-NOTE
🔴 IMPORTANT
This provision concerns possession of counterfeit currency-related instruments with the
requisite:

    ●   knowledge/reason to believe that it is counterfeit; and
    ●   intention to use it as genuine.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 218 / 748>
⭐ THREE ELEMENTS
Remember:

POSSESSION + KNOWLEDGE + INTENTION
All three are important.




Example
A knowingly keeps 50 counterfeit ₹500 notes with the intention of introducing them into
circulation.

This is much more serious than accidentally possessing one counterfeit note without knowing it
was fake.

SECTION 181 – MAKING OR POSSESSING
INSTRUMENTS/MATERIALS FOR
COUNTERFEITING
🔴 IMPORTANT
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 219 / 748>
The law also punishes people who prepare the means of counterfeiting.

This can include:

   ●   machinery;
   ●   instruments;
   ●   materials;
   ●   equipment;
   ●   other means designed for counterfeiting.




Example
A manufactures specialised printing plates designed to produce fake bank-notes.

Even before large-scale fake notes are circulated, the preparation of such instruments may
constitute an offence under §181.




⭐ CONCEPT
The law does not wait until the counterfeit note reaches circulation.

It also criminalises the preparation and possession of specialised means for
counterfeiting.

   ●   resemble currency-notes or bank-notes; and

⭐ EXAM-ORIENTED PROBLEM
Question
A receives a counterfeit ₹500 note from B.

A genuinely believes it is genuine.

Later A discovers that it is counterfeit but nevertheless uses it to purchase goods from C.

Answer
The crucial issue is A's knowledge at the time of use.

Initially, mere receipt without knowledge does not automatically establish the offence.

Once A knows that it is counterfeit and nevertheless uses it as genuine, the ingredients of the
relevant provision concerning use/dealing in counterfeit currency as genuine may be
established.




⭐ ANOTHER PROBLEM
A knowingly keeps 100 counterfeit notes hidden in his house, intending to circulate them.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 226 / 748>
Answer
The facts indicate:

   ●    possession;
   ●    knowledge;
   ●    intention to use as genuine.

Therefore, the offence relating to possession of counterfeit currency with requisite
intention may apply.




⭐ ANOTHER PROBLEM
A creates a specialised printing plate solely designed to manufacture counterfeit bank-notes.

No counterfeit note has yet been circulated.

Answer
The fact that circulation has not yet occurred does not necessarily prevent prosecution.

The law separately addresses making/possessing instruments or materials for
counterfeiting.
