🔴statement
    Q43. A witness knowingly gives a false
           in court after taking an oath.
What offence?
Answer
BNS §227 – Giving false evidence.

A is legally bound to speak the truth and knowingly gives false evidence.

Conclusion
A may be guilty of giving false evidence.

UNIT X – FORGERY

🔴document
    Q45. A creates a fake electronic
           intending to deceive B. What
provisions may become relevant?
Answer
The starting point is BNS §335, concerning making a false document or false electronic record.

If the additional statutory requirements of forgery are satisfied, §336 may apply.

Depending upon the nature of the document, aggravated provisions such as §§337–338 may
become relevant.

Conclusion
Electronic records are expressly incorporated into the BNS framework.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 688 / 748>
🔴forged
    Q46. A knows that a document is
        but presents it in court as genuine.
What offence may arise?
Answer
BNS §340 deals with using a forged document/electronic record as genuine.

The prosecution must establish that:

   ●   the document/e-record is forged;
   ●   A knew or had the requisite knowledge regarding its forged character;
   ●   A used it as genuine.

Conclusion
A may be liable under §340.




UNIT XI – CRIMINAL INTIMIDATION

🔴me,Q47.  A tells B, “If you testify against
     I will kill you.” What offence may
arise?
Answer
BNS §351 – Criminal intimidation.

There is:

   ●   threat of injury;
   ●   threat to B's person;
   ●   intention to compel B to refrain from a legally relevant act.

Conclusion
A may be liable for criminal intimidation, with the aggravated punishment provisions considered
according to the nature of the threat.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 689 / 748>
🔴butQ48. A insults B during an argument,
    there is no intention to cause alarm or
compel B to act. Is every insult criminal
intimidation?
Answer
No.

Criminal intimidation requires the statutory ingredients of a threat and the requisite intention.

A mere insult may potentially fall under §352 if its own ingredients are satisfied, but it does not
automatically constitute intimidation.

⭐ Distinction
Threat → §351

Intentional insult provoking breach of peace → §352




UNIT XII – DEFAMATION

🔴B isQ49. A publishes a false statement that
      a corrupt businessman, intending to
damage B's reputation. What offence may
arise?
Answer
BNS §356 – Defamation.

The prosecution must establish:
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 690 / 748>
      1.   imputation concerning B;
      2.   making/publication;
      3.   requisite intention/knowledge regarding harm to reputation;
      4.   absence of applicable statutory exception.

Conclusion
If established, A may be guilty of criminal defamation.




🔴aboutQ50.a public
            A makes a truthful statement
                   servant's conduct while
performing public functions. Is A
automatically guilty of defamation?
Answer
No.

The statutory exceptions to defamation must be considered.

The first exception concerns truth when publication is for the public good, and other
exceptions concern good-faith opinions regarding public servants and public questions.

Conclusion
A may fall within a statutory exception, depending upon the facts.
