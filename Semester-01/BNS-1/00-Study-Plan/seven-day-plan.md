# BNS-1 — 7-Day Exam Study Plan

**Approach:** Beginner-first, exam-oriented, simple English.  
**Suggested intensity:** 5–6 focused study hours daily, split into sessions. Adapt this plan to the time remaining and your exam timetable.

| Day | Coverage from syllabus | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concept and elements of crime; stages; strict and constructive liability | Definitions, comparison charts, two long answers |
| 2 | Common intention, common object; abetment, conspiracy and attempt | Section map, comparisons and problem questions |
| 3 | General exceptions: mistake, accident, necessity, young age, unsoundness of mind, drunkenness, private defence | Exception checklist and illustrations |
| 4 | Human-body offences: culpable homicide, murder, death by rash/negligent act, hurt/grievous hurt, restraint/confinement, kidnapping/abduction, assault/criminal force | Offence ingredient tables and distinctions |
| 5 | Property offences: theft, extortion, robbery, dacoity, dishonest misappropriation, criminal breach of trust and cheating | Comparison tables and five problem questions |
| 6 | Offences against State/public tranquillity and offences against women/children listed by CCSU | Topic checklist and long-answer outlines |
| 7 | Whole-syllabus revision and mock exam | Timed answers, error log, final section review |

## Daily routine
1. **First session:** Read the topic in simple English.
2. **Second session:** Check the exact statutory text and mark section numbers.
3. **Third session:** Write one long answer and two short notes without looking.
4. **Fourth session:** Answer practice questions and update an error log.
5. **Last 15 minutes:** Recall section numbers, distinctions and exceptions.

## Important study priorities
- Complete every topic in the official syllabus at least once.
- Learn statutory ingredients before memorising lengthy explanations.
- Use accurate legal distinctions; do not assume similar offences are interchangeable.
- Practise answer structure: Introduction → statutory provision → ingredients → explanation → illustration → case law if relevant → conclusion.
- Never rely on a predicted question as a substitute for syllabus coverage.
