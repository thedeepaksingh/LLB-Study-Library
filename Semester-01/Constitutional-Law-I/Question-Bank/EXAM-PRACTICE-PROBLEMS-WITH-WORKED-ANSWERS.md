# K-1002 — Original Practice Questions with Worked Answers
**These exercises are original. They are not verified CCSU PYQs.**

## Part A — Essay questions with answer structures

### PRACTICE-CON-01. Discuss the nature of the Indian Constitution and its federal features.
Define federalism; explain written/supreme Constitution, division of powers, State governments, independent judiciary and special amendment requirements; contrast centralising features; use *S.R. Bommai*; conclude that India combines federal and centralising elements.

### PRACTICE-CON-02. Explain the significance of the Preamble.
List its key terms and ideals; explain interpretive importance and *Kesavananda Bharati*; state that the Preamble is not usually a standalone enforceable right; link enforceable claims to a substantive Article.

### PRACTICE-CON-03. Explain Article 14.
Define equality before law and equal protection; explain reasonable classification; mention arbitrariness under *E.P. Royappa*; apply the appropriate test rather than treating every distinction as forbidden.

### PRACTICE-CON-04. Discuss freedom of speech and expression.
Explain Article 19(1)(a), citizen status, scope and Article 19(2) grounds. Include a relevant case such as *Shreya Singhal* and assess the challenged restriction in context.

### PRACTICE-CON-05. Explain the three protections under Article 20.
Discuss each clause separately: ex post facto criminal law, double jeopardy and compelled self-incrimination. State the constitutional conditions and avoid treating each as identical.

### PRACTICE-CON-06. Trace the development of Article 21.
Start with text; explain *Maneka Gandhi* and fair procedure; add privacy/dignity or custodial safeguards using a precise case; recognise that each claimed right has a developed doctrinal test.

### PRACTICE-CON-07. Explain Articles 23 and 24.
Distinguish forced labour/trafficking from prohibited employment of children; explain who is bound and the factual conditions; add the correct case/statutory context.

### PRACTICE-CON-08. Explain freedom of religion under Articles 25–28.
Separate individual freedom, denominational management, taxation and institutional instruction; discuss constitutional restrictions; apply the exact Article.

### PRACTICE-CON-09. Explain the relationship between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs.
Set out Parts III/IV, Article 37, the history from *Champakam Dorairajan* and later harmonious balance in *Minerva Mills*.

### PRACTICE-CON-10. Explain Article 32 and writ remedies.
Define the right to approach the Supreme Court; accurately distinguish each writ; compare with Article 226 without saying the two jurisdictions are identical.

## Part B — Fact patterns

### Problem 1: A speech restriction
A State order bans a peaceful criticism of government policy, citing only that the criticism might embarrass officials.
**Worked answer:** Start with Article 19(1)(a), available to citizens. Embarrassment by itself is not one of the listed Article 19(2) grounds. Identify the law and evidence and ask whether the restriction is authorised by law, relates to a listed ground and satisfies the applicable constitutional test. Use relevant speech authority, but do not decide the matter on the word “peaceful” alone; the full context and actual grounds matter.

### Problem 2: A classification
A scholarship is given only to students from a defined rural district on the ground that educational access in that district is substantially lower.
**Worked answer:** Article 14 applies. Ask whether the classification has an intelligible differentia and whether it has a rational relation to the scholarship's legitimate objective. Evidence about educational disadvantage is relevant. The classification is not automatically invalid merely because students elsewhere are excluded, nor automatically valid merely because the government announces a purpose. Check any other applicable equality provisions and the programme's actual design.

### Problem 3: A law enacted after conduct
An act was lawful when done. A later criminal statute seeks to punish the act and impose a greater penalty retroactively.
**Worked answer:** Article 20(1) directly raises protection against retrospective criminalisation and a greater retrospective penalty. Identify dates, offence definition and punishment imposed. Do not use Article 20(2) because this is not a repeat-prosecution problem.

### Problem 4: Forced work
A contractor retains workers' identity documents, threatens them with violence, and makes them work while withholding legally required wages.
**Worked answer:** Analyse Article 23 and the statutory context, including whether the conditions constitute forced labour under the governing doctrine. Relevant threats, inability to leave, wage conditions and control are material. *People's Union for Democratic Rights* is an important authority, but the result depends on actual facts and the precise legal claim.

### Problem 5: Religion and a public institution
A publicly funded educational institution requires all students to participate in devotional worship for one religion.
**Worked answer:** Articles 25 and 28 may be relevant, but identify the institution's constitutional category, the type of funding/control, the nature of the instruction and the individual's conscience claim. Do not automatically assume Article 28 applies identically to every school. Quote the clause and apply its scope.

### Problem 6: A public servant's detention
A person is detained without being informed of the grounds or brought through the required process.
**Worked answer:** Consider Article 21 and the related constitutional provisions governing arrest/detention, plus the current statute and facts. *Maneka Gandhi* and *D.K. Basu* can guide fair-procedure analysis. State whether the question concerns arrest as an accused, preventive detention or another form of custody because different rules apply.

### Problem 7: A minority educational institution
A regulator imposes academic-quality standards on a minority institution and also seeks to control its selection of internal staff without explaining any quality rationale.
**Worked answer:** Article 30 protects the right to establish and administer minority educational institutions, but administration is not necessarily immune from every regulation. Separate permissible quality regulation from interference that may impair the protected right. Use *St. Xavier's College* and *T.M.A. Pai Foundation* with reference to the precise issue and relevant current doctrine.
