# K-1002 — Constitutional Law Casebook
**Format:** facts/issue in brief, principle, exam use. Verify the reported judgment before relying on a detailed citation.

## Preamble, federalism and constitutional limits

### 1. Kesavananda Bharati v State of Kerala (1973)
**Issue:** Scope of Parliament's power to amend the Constitution.  
**Principle:** Parliament's Article 368 power is wide but cannot destroy the Constitution's basic structure. The Preamble is part of the Constitution and assists in understanding its foundational commitments.  
**Use:** Preamble, constitutional supremacy, amendment limits. Do not claim every constitutional provision is unamendable.

### 2. S.R. Bommai v Union of India (1994)
**Issue:** Use of Article 356 and limits on dismissing a State government.  
**Principle:** Proclamation under Article 356 is subject to judicial review; the judgment is also a leading authority for secularism and federalism as basic-structure principles.  
**Use:** Federal structure and the legal limits on central power. Tie the proposition to the issue rather than saying all political choices are justiciable.

### 3. Minerva Mills Ltd. v Union of India (1980)
**Issue:** Constitutional amendment that sought to enlarge Parliament's amending power and alter the balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs.  
**Principle:** The Court invalidated the impugned provisions and stressed limited amending power and constitutional balance.  
**Use:** Relationship between Parts III and IV and basic structure.

### 4. State of Madras v Champakam Dorairajan (1951)
**Issue:** Whether a communal allocation of educational seats conflicted with Fundamental Rights.  
**Principle:** The Court treated Fundamental Rights as controlling in the conflict presented, contributing to the First Amendment and later constitutional development.  
**Use:** Historical starting point for Fundamental Rights/DPSP relationships. Explain subsequent developments; do not stop at 1951.

## Equality and speech

### 5. E.P. Royappa v State of Tamil Nadu (1974)
**Issue:** Arbitrary treatment in a public-service context and Article 14.  
**Principle:** The Court associated arbitrariness with inequality, developing Article 14 beyond a narrowly formal reading of classification.  
**Use:** Article 14. A complete answer still applies the relevant current doctrine to facts.

### 6. Maneka Gandhi v Union of India (1978)
**Issue:** Passport impounding and personal liberty.  
**Principle:** Procedure affecting Article 21 liberty must be fair, just and reasonable; Articles 14, 19 and 21 are read together in the doctrine developed by the Court.  
**Use:** Articles 14/21, fair procedure and constitutional interpretation.

### 7. Shreya Singhal v Union of India (2015)
**Issue:** Constitutionality of former Information Technology Act section 66A and other online-speech provisions.  
**Principle:** Section 66A was struck down for violating Article 19(1)(a), among other reasons discussed in the judgment; the Court distinguished discussion, advocacy and incitement in its analysis of speech restrictions.  
**Use:** Article 19(1)(a), vagueness and restrictions. Describe the former statutory provision accurately; do not treat the case as approving every current restriction.

### 8. Romesh Thappar v State of Madras (1950)
**Issue:** A ban on circulation of a journal and freedom of expression under the original constitutional text.  
**Principle:** The Court closely scrutinised restrictions on speech in the constitutional framework then applicable. The case is historically important; later constitutional amendments altered Article 19(2).  
**Use:** Early Article 19 doctrine, while expressly acknowledging the later text.

## Life, liberty and protection in criminal process

### 9. Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v Union of India (2017)
**Issue:** Whether privacy is protected by the Constitution.  
**Principle:** A nine-judge bench recognised privacy as a fundamental right rooted in liberty, dignity and constitutional guarantees.  
**Use:** Article 21 and modern rights jurisprudence. Distinguish the 2017 privacy decision from later cases applying it.

### 10. D.K. Basu v State of West Bengal (1997)
**Issue:** Custodial abuse and safeguards during arrest/detention.  
**Principle:** The Court laid down procedural safeguards intended to reduce custodial abuse and protect constitutional rights.  
**Use:** Article 21 and police custody. Do not substitute the judgment for the current statutory arrest provisions.

### 11. Bijoe Emmanuel v State of Kerala (1986)
**Issue:** Schoolchildren who respectfully stood but did not sing the national anthem due to sincere religious belief.  
**Principle:** The Court protected the students' freedom of conscience/expression on the facts and rejected punishment unsupported by lawful requirements.  
**Use:** Article 19(1)(a), conscience and constitutional tolerance. Explain the factual setting and limits.

## Exploitation, religion and minority education

### 12. People's Union for Democratic Rights v Union of India (1982)
**Issue:** Labour conditions and the constitutional prohibition of forced labour in a public works project.  
**Principle:** The Court interpreted Article 23 in a way that addresses economic compulsion and the relevant failure to pay legally required minimum wages in the context before it.  
**Use:** Article 23. Carefully describe the factual and doctrinal conditions; avoid treating every poor employment choice as automatically forced labour.

### 13. Commissioner, Hindu Religious Endowments, Madras v Sri Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar of Sri Shirur Mutt (1954)
**Issue:** Religious denomination and governmental regulation.  
**Principle:** The case is a foundational Article 25–26 decision on the scope of religious freedom and denominational management.  
**Use:** Discuss the constitutional treatment of religious practices and institutional affairs. Use precise doctrine from the judgment rather than slogans about an “essential religious practice” test.

### 14. St. Xavier's College v State of Gujarat (1974)
**Issue:** Regulation of minority educational institutions.  
**Principle:** The Court considered Article 30 rights and limits on regulatory control; regulation may be permissible when consistent with the institution's protected character.  
**Use:** Article 30, minority rights and quality/accountability regulation.

### 15. T.M.A. Pai Foundation v State of Karnataka (2002)
**Issue:** Rights of private educational institutions, including minority institutions.  
**Principle:** The eleven-judge bench addressed institution establishment/administration and permitted regulation consistent with the constitutional framework; precise holdings vary by issue.  
**Use:** Articles 29–30. Read the relevant part of the judgment rather than assigning the whole decision one simple slogan.

## DPSPs and constitutional values
The earlier cases above provide context for balancing rights, governance and social objectives. Pair *Champakam Dorairajan* with *Minerva Mills* to show how doctrine developed over time. A good answer distinguishes the non-justiciability of DPSPs from their constitutional importance under Article 37.

## Citation discipline
Use party names and years in exam notes, but before formal writing check the report citation, bench, issue and ratio in a reliable report. An accurate doctrine explained clearly is better than an invented pinpoint citation.
